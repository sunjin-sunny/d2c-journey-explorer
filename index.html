Behind this Project

How I built a global D2C journey explorer with AI

A personal experiment in product thinking, customer journey design, generative AI and vibe coding.
Built from concept to working prototype without a traditional development background.

3 Markets
3 Personas
12 Customer Moments
3 Product Categories
AI Co-built
0 Frameworks

  1. I started with a customer journey, not an org chart.

    The original question was simple: what actually happens behind a customer's purchase journey? Instead of explaining teams through a traditional organization chart, I designed three fictional customers and let each person experience a different shopping journey.

  2. One platform, three very different purchase contexts.

    Alex shops for a Galaxy device in the United States, Matthew uses an AI shopping assistant to choose a TV in the United Kingdom, and Vanessa buys an air conditioner in Mexico. The journeys reveal how market, category and customer context change the experience.

  3. Each screen became a window into a capability.

    Instead of annotating every detail, I added one interactive pointer to each key moment. Clicking it reveals the product, experience, technology or commercial capability behind what the customer sees. The goal was to make a complex D2C ecosystem understandable through interaction rather than explanation.

  4. AI became a product partner, not just a writing tool.

    I used AI throughout the process to challenge the concept, structure the journey, write and refine interface copy, debug JavaScript, review screenshots and generate visual assets. The workflow felt closer to pairing with a designer, engineer and editor at the same time than simply prompting a chatbot.

  5. The personas were designed as part of the product experience.

    Alex, Matthew and Vanessa were created as pixel-art characters with different lifestyles, motivations and shopping needs. Their full persona sheets are optional, while the main experience stays lightweight: meet the customer, then follow the journey.

  6. I deliberately kept the build simple.

    The prototype uses HTML, CSS and JavaScript without a front-end framework. Journey content is separated into a data layer, so screens, copy, pointers and personas can be changed without rebuilding the interaction logic.

  7. The hardest part was deciding what not to show.

    The first version tried to explain too much. Extra screens, detailed organizational structures and supporting information made the journey harder to understand. Iteration gradually turned the project into something simpler: one customer moment, one insight, one clear reason to keep exploring.

  8. What I learned

    AI lowers the cost of building, but it does not remove the need for product judgment. The most important decisions were still about what problem to explain, what information to remove, how the journey should flow and what the customer should understand next.

Built as an experiment in combining product management, AI and hands-on prototyping.
